# Commercial Intelligence and AI-Enabled Marketing Operations — B2B

**Setting:** specialised, high-value B2B expertise business  
**Period:** 2026-present  
**Status:** ongoing work; commercial outcomes are not claimed

## The context

In a specialised B2B market, useful commercial information is often fragmented. Market activity, organisations, professional roles, timing and internal expertise need to be understood together before deciding where marketing attention may be useful.

The challenge is not simply collecting more information. It is deciding what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain and what should be tested before the business invests further.

## What I contribute

My work connects three areas:

### Commercial intelligence

I gather and structure market, organisation and opportunity evidence so that commercial decisions begin from something more reliable than assumptions.

I distinguish between:

- verified information;
- evidence-backed interpretation;
- a hypothesis that still needs validation;
- an open question.

This helps prevent an interesting research finding from automatically becoming a sales claim.

### Marketing operations

I connect research with the work that follows it: positioning, content, digital implementation, review, measurement and learning.

The purpose is to make marketing easier to maintain and improve, rather than producing disconnected outputs that lose their context after delivery.

### Human-governed AI

AI supports research, synthesis, comparison, drafting, documentation and implementation. I remain responsible for the commercial question, evidence standard, judgement, corrections and decisions about what should be used.

For me, AI-enabled does not mean autonomous. It means the workflow is designed to use AI productively while keeping human review and accountability.

## How I approach the work

1. Begin with the decision the business needs to make.
2. Gather relevant internal and external evidence.
3. Separate facts from interpretation and uncertainty.
4. connect market demand with what the business can genuinely deliver.
5. Choose a responsible next action that can produce useful learning.
6. Carry the learning back into the marketing system.
