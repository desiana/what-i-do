# Full-Stack Digital Marketing — Earlier Client and Business Work

**Work type:** independent business and client marketing  
**Status:** implemented work with one bounded measured campaign comparison  
**Privacy:** client identities and private customer material excluded  
**Last reviewed:** 16 September 2026

## Overview

This earlier work shows the execution foundation beneath my current strategy and systems work.

As a business owner and hands-on marketer, I did not encounter marketing as a set of isolated departments. I had to understand the audience, decide the positioning, create the assets, operate the channels, support the sales conversation and examine what happened next.

That experience is why I describe myself as cross-functional rather than as a specialist in only one channel.

## Business-based marketing evidence

### Consumer-product e-commerce

The marketing work was broader than operating an online store. I evaluated market response, changed the product direction, differentiated the offer and repositioned it toward a mid-to-high market. I also connected traffic, conversion, customer service, reviews, sourcing and distribution rather than treating them as separate activities.

Earlier business records attribute the following results to the combined marketing and operational changes:

- a fivefold increase in revenue following niche-market repositioning;
- a 37% reduction in overhead after the production workflow was reviewed;
- a 16% improvement in net income following vendor and distribution changes;
- growth to a 4.8-star rating across more than 2,000 reviews.

These are historical self-reported business records. The original dashboards and calculation methods are not preserved, so the figures should be presented with their context rather than as independently audited results.

### Food-service business

The central marketing decision was not simply how to advertise the existing restaurant. I changed the route to market: the business moved away from relying on dine-in traffic and increasingly operated through takeaway and an early food-delivery platform.

The work also included offer and experience design through in-house recipes, a more focused menu and closer alignment between what customers could order and what the operation could deliver efficiently. Earlier records describe the business as becoming one of the delivery platform's stronger-selling local merchants; that remains a historical business record rather than a third-party-verified ranking.

### First e-commerce venture

I built the website and handled the digital-commercial work, but the more important result was recognising that the product did not fit the intended market well enough.

This was a costly lesson in product-market fit before I had formal language for lean validation. It now informs how I work: enthusiasm for a product is not evidence of demand, and stopping or changing direction can be a better commercial decision than continuing to promote the wrong offer.

### Early local-service business

This experience taught me about capacity, business-model economics and the difference between owning a business and owning a scalable system. It is useful background, but the available evidence does not support presenting it as a substantial marketing case. I would include it only when the business history or transferable operational learning is relevant.

## 1. Customer and audience understanding

I conducted audience research by:

- listening first to the client's own community;
- expanding into relevant online groups and forums;
- examining competitor positioning and popular content;
- observing how the client engaged with customers;
- mapping pains, goals, interests and objections;
- using the findings to inform customer personas, content and value propositions.

The principle was simple: content strategy should begin with the audience and business objective, not with copying whatever competitors publish.

## 2. Customer journeys and measurement

I mapped customer journeys into smaller steps and attached indicators to different funnel stages.

Examples included:

- awareness indicators such as visitors, reach or followers;
- consideration indicators such as messages, wish-list activity or resource engagement;
- conversion indicators such as products added to cart or appointments booked.

I also improved customer experience through clearer profile bios, better content or product categorisation, evolving website copy and customer-centred content.

This work taught me to connect channels to the actual customer journey rather than judging marketing only by surface engagement.

## 3. Paid-media experimentation

One documented advertising comparison changed the age targeting and creative framing.

Compared with the previous variation, the documented result was:

- **24% greater reach**;
- **21% more views**;
- **25% lower cost per follow**.

The wider funnel analysis also identified an important distinction: the advertisement promoted a free resource and captured leads, but meaningful conversion often depended on a follow-up conversation with new followers.

This is one bounded campaign comparison. It is not presented as a general revenue or client-growth claim.

## 4. Content production and distribution

My hands-on work included:

- product photography and video;
- collaboration with professional studios;
- co-created remote video production;
- image, carousel, story and short-video content;
- editing with Canva, CapCut, Photoshop and Lightroom;
- scheduling and publishing through native and third-party tools;
- adapting creative style to the audience rather than assuming that polished content was always best.

I worked from research and business goals through production and publication, rather than treating content creation as an isolated task.

## 5. Customer engagement and sales support

I:

- responded to positive and negative feedback;
- assisted customers through direct messages;
- developed structured chat frameworks for client conversations;
- used public engagement as part of brand reputation, not merely as a private reply;
- supported lead nurturing after content and advertising created attention.

This work connected marketing communication with customer experience and sales progression.

## 6. Websites, funnels and commerce

My work extended beyond social media into:

- lead-magnet and lead-capture journeys;
- landing pages and nurturing sequences;
- e-commerce websites;
- WordPress and Divi implementation;
- GoHighLevel workflows;
- copy and usability improvements;
- conversion-oriented customer paths.

The common thread was building the path from discovery to action rather than producing disconnected marketing assets.

## 7. Early AI-assisted marketing

I created structured “AI twin” profiles from existing business material, including:

- writing style and structure;
- tone of voice;
- repeated phrases and terminology;
- philosophy, mission and offering;
- ideal clients and common scepticism.

I used these profiles to support consistent writing and empathetic customer responses while keeping the business's existing material as the source.

This work anticipated my later focus on governed AI and knowledge systems: AI performs better when context, evidence and boundaries are deliberately structured.
